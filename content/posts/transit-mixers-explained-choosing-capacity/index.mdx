Transit mixers rarely get the attention cranes and boom lifts do, but getting capacity and scheduling wrong on this equipment directly causes one of the most common site delays: concrete arriving too slowly, or in volumes that don't match the pour sequence.

## What a Transit Mixer Does

A transit mixer is a truck-mounted rotating drum that transports ready-mix concrete from a batching plant to a construction site while continuously agitating the mix to prevent the concrete from setting or segregating in transit. Timing matters here: concrete has a limited workable window from the moment it's batched, so reliable, well-scheduled transit mixer delivery is directly tied to pour quality, not just logistics convenience.

## Capacity Range Explained (6m³–10m³)

Our transit mixer fleet covers **6 to 10 cubic metres** of capacity per unit, which comfortably spans the range needed for most Indian construction projects, from smaller structural pours to large-scale slab and foundation work. Capacity selection should be driven by your actual pour volume and schedule, not simply "the biggest truck available," since larger units also need correspondingly better site access to maneuver and position for discharge.

## Matching Capacity to Pour Volume and Site Access

- **Calculate total pour volume first**, then plan the number of mixer loads and delivery intervals needed to maintain a continuous pour without cold joints forming between loads.
- **Confirm site access** for the mixer size being considered. Larger trucks need adequate road width and turning space to reach the discharge point.
- **Sequence deliveries against your actual placement rate**, not just truck availability. A mixer arriving faster than concrete can be placed creates its own problems, including the risk of the load beginning to set before use.
- **Build in a buffer for traffic and distance from the batching plant**, particularly for projects where concrete travel time is a meaningful share of the available workable window.

## FAQs

**What capacity transit mixer do I need for a typical slab pour?**
This depends entirely on your pour volume and schedule; calculate total concrete volume and desired pour rate first, then size mixer capacity and delivery frequency to match.

**Why does transit mixer timing matter so much?**
Concrete has a limited workable window after batching. Delayed or poorly sequenced deliveries risk the concrete beginning to set before proper placement and finishing.

**Can transit mixers access tight urban sites?**
It depends on the specific site's road width and turning space; larger-capacity units need correspondingly more room to maneuver, which is worth confirming before booking.

**Does GPS tracking actually improve pour scheduling?**
Yes, real-time location visibility lets site teams adjust placement crew timing based on actual delivery status rather than estimated arrival alone, reducing both idle time and rushed placement.
