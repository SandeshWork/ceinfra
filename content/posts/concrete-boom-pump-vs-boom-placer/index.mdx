Concrete boom pumps and boom placers both move wet concrete from ground level to the pour point, but the equipment, mobility, and ideal use case differ enough that picking the wrong one can slow down a pour schedule considerably. Here's how to tell them apart.

## What's the Difference?

A **concrete boom pump** is typically a truck-mounted unit that both pumps concrete under pressure through a pipeline and positions it via a folding boom arm, combining transport, pumping, and placement in one mobile machine. A **boom placer** is generally a fixed or semi-fixed unit, often tower-mounted or structure-mounted on larger sites, that receives concrete from a separate pump and distributes it across a wide working radius via its own rotating boom, without the pumping and mobility functions built into the same unit.

In practice, the boom pump brings its own pumping power and mobility to a site; the boom placer is a distribution tool that extends the reach of concrete already being pumped to the pour location.

## Reach & Rotation Compared

- **Boom pumps** in our fleet reach **28 to 61 metres** vertically, with boom articulation allowing placement around obstacles on an active site.
- **Boom placers** cover a **24-to-42-metre reach** with full **360-degree rotation** from a fixed or semi-fixed position, making them effective for continuously covering a wide pour area without repositioning the entire unit.

The practical difference: a boom pump suits a site where the pour location changes and the machine needs to reposition between moves, while a boom placer suits a large, continuous pour area that can be covered efficiently by rotating around a single fixed point.

## Best For: High-Rise vs. Site-Fixed Pours

**Boom pumps** are generally the better fit for high-rise construction, where the unit can be positioned at street level and the boom extended to reach upper floors as the structure rises, offering flexibility as the pour location changes floor by floor.

**Boom placers** suit large, site-fixed pours, such as extensive slab or raft foundation work, where a single, strategically positioned unit can rotate through 360 degrees to cover the full working area without needing to relocate.

## Choosing Capacity for Your Project

- Confirm the vertical and horizontal reach actually required at your furthest pour point, not just the building's overall height
- Account for site access and ground conditions where the unit will be positioned, particularly for larger truck-mounted boom pumps
- For boom placers, confirm mounting requirements, since larger units are often structure-mounted and need to be planned into the construction sequence itself
- Match pumping output and reach together. A unit with sufficient reach but insufficient output will still slow down a large pour

## FAQs

**Can a boom placer pump concrete on its own?**
Typically no. Boom placers generally distribute concrete supplied by a separate pump rather than pumping it themselves, unlike boom pumps, which combine both functions.

**Which has greater reach, a boom pump or a boom placer?**
In our fleet, boom pumps reach further vertically (up to 61 metres) than boom placers (up to 42 metres), though boom placers offer full 360-degree rotational coverage from a fixed point.

**Are boom pumps suitable for high-rise construction?**
Yes, their mobility and ability to reposition as a building rises make them a common choice for high-rise pours, positioned at street level with the boom reaching upper floors.

**What determines the right reach for my project?**
The actual distance from where the pumping equipment can be positioned to the furthest point requiring concrete placement, not the building's overall footprint or height alone.
